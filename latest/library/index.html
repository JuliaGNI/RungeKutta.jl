RungeKutta.PartitionedTableau — Type

Tableau of a Partitioned Runge-Kutta method

\[\begin{aligned} Q_{n,i} &= q_{n} + h \sum \limits_{j=1}^{s} a_{ij} \, v(t_{n} + c_j \Delta t, Q_{n,j}, P_{n,j}) , & q_{n+1} &= q_{n} + h \sum \limits_{i=1}^{s} b_{i} \, v(t_{n} + c_j \Delta t, Q_{n,i}, P_{n,i}) , \\ P_{n,i} &= p_{n} + h \sum \limits_{i=1}^{s} \bar{a}_{ij} \, f(t_{n} + c_j \Delta t, Q_{n,j}, P_{n,j}) , & p_{n+1} &= p_{n} + h \sum \limits_{i=1}^{s} \bar{b}_{i} \, f(t_{n} + c_j \Delta t, Q_{n,i}, P_{n,i}) . \end{aligned}\]

RungeKutta.StabilityFunction — Type
StabilityFunction{T}

The stability function $R(z) = P(z) / Q(z)$ of a Runge-Kutta tableau, the factor by which one step multiplies the solution of $\dot{y} = λ y$ with $z = h λ$. The fields P and Q are Vector{T}s of length $s + 1$, the coefficients of $P(z) = \det(I - z A + z 𝟙 b^T)$ and $Q(z) = \det(I - z A)$ in ascending powers of $z$; none of them is trimmed or rounded.

R(z) evaluates the quotient P(z) / Q(z) for a real or complex z, as computed, also at a pole.

See stability_function.

RungeKutta._nullvector — Method

Compute a vector spanning the one-dimensional nullspace of the square matrix A.

Uses a column-pivoted QR factorisation, which unlike the singular value decomposition is implemented generically in LinearAlgebra and thus works for arbitrary floating point types such as BigFloat. With A * P = Q * R and A of rank n-1, the last diagonal entry of R vanishes, so z = [y; 1] with R[1:n-1,1:n-1] * y = -R[1:n-1,n] spans the nullspace of R and P * z that of A. The result is normalised to unit length and its first nonzero entry is made positive, so that the vector is determined by A alone and does not depend on the pivot order.

A must be at least 2×2 and of rank exactly n-1, otherwise an ArgumentError is thrown rather than a vector returned that spans nothing. Pivoting orders the diagonal of R by decreasing magnitude, so that is the case precisely when the last entry has collapsed relative to the first and the one before it has not. For the matrices this is used on the two are separated by some seventy orders of magnitude, so the exact threshold does not matter.

RungeKutta.imaginary_stability_interval — Method
imaginary_stability_interval(tab::PartitionedTableau{T}; rtol) where {T <: Real}

Return the supremum of the $r ≥ 0$ such that the amplification matrix $M(ν)$ of one step on the harmonic oscillator $\dot{q} = p$, $\dot{p} = -ω^2 q$, with $ν = h ω$, the tableau tab.q acting on $\dot{q}$ and the tableau tab.p on $\dot{p}$, has spectral radius at most 1 for every $ν ∈ [0, r]$; of type float(T), and Inf where no bound exists. For a tableau paired with itself, it equals the interval of that tableau, up to round-off for a floating-point T.

The spectral radius of the real $2 × 2$ matrix $M$ is at most 1 exactly when $|\det M| ≤ 1$, $\det(I + M) ≥ 0$ and $\det(I - M) ≥ 0$. With $\det M = N_1/D$ and $\det(I + M) = N_2/D$ as quotients of polynomials in $ν$, these are the polynomial conditions $D^2 - N_1^2 ≥ 0$, $D N_2 ≥ 0$ and $D (2D + 2N_1 - N_2) ≥ 0$, each decided as in real_stability_interval, and the interval ends at the first point where one of them turns negative. For a floating-point T, the coefficients of $D$, $N_1$ and $N_2$ come from the Faddeev–LeVerrier recursion with a first-order bound on their round-off, for entries of the tableaus that carry a relative error rtol, and none of them counts as zero. A coefficient of a condition $g$ counts as zero when its magnitude is at most its bound, which the products and sums that make $g$ carry on as in isastable. With the factor $ν^j$ of its lowest nonzero coefficient divided out, a root where $g$ changes its sign ends the interval only where $g$ falls below $-\sum_k e_k ν^k$, with $e_k$ the bound of its coefficient $k$, at the midpoint between that root and the next, or is negative beyond the last root: a point where a condition touches zero, such as $\det(I + M) = 0$ at $M = -I$ for the Gauss tableaus, then does not end the interval when round-off splits it into two close roots. The default rtol is 16 * eps(T) for a floating-point T and 0 for a rational one, where every decision is exact.

RungeKutta.imaginary_stability_interval — Method
imaginary_stability_interval(tab::Tableau{T}; rtol) where {T <: Real}

Return the supremum of the $r ≥ 0$ with $|R(iy)| ≤ 1$ for every $y ∈ [-r, r]$, of type float(T), and Inf where no bound exists. A point where $|R| = 1$ and $|R| ≤ 1$ on both sides does not end the interval, and an interval where $|R(iy)| > 1$ for every small $y ≠ 0$ is exactly 0.

It is decided from the coefficients of $R = P/Q$, by the sign of $E(y) = |Q(iy)|^2 - |P(iy)|^2$ on $y > 0$ as in real_stability_interval, with the same zero count, the same bounds, the same test of a touching point and the same default rtol.

RungeKutta.isalgebraicallystable — Method
isalgebraicallystable(tab::Tableau{T}; rtol) where {T <: Real}

Return whether tab is algebraically stable: every weight $b_i ≥ 0$, and the algebraic_stability_matrix $M$ is positive semidefinite. The semidefinite test is a symmetric elimination that pivots on the largest diagonal entry; $M$ is positive semidefinite when each pivot is positive until the remaining block is zero.

The decision is exact for a rational T. For a floating-point T, a weight, a pivot or an entry of the remaining block counts as zero when its magnitude is at most $\mathrm{rtol} \cdot m$, with $m$ the largest magnitude of an entry of $M$ and $b$. The default rtol is 16 * eps(T) for a floating-point T and 0 for a rational one. Returns a Bool.

RungeKutta.isastable — Method
isastable(tab::Tableau{T}; rtol) where {T <: Real}

Return whether tab is A-stable, that is whether $|R(z)| ≤ 1$ for every $z$ with $\mathrm{Re}\, z ≤ 0$. It is decided from the coefficients of $R = P/Q$ without sampling $z$, by two conditions:

  1. $Q$ has no zero with $\mathrm{Re}\, z ≤ 0$ (the Routh–Hurwitz test of $Q(-z)$);
  2. $E(y) = |Q(iy)|^2 - |P(iy)|^2 ≥ 0$ for every real $y$ (a sign count of the polynomial $E$ on $y > 0$, by its square-free factorisation and a Sturm sequence).

Together they are sufficient for A-stability, and equivalent to it where $P$ and $Q$ have no common zero. A zero of $Q$ with $\mathrm{Re}\, z ≤ 0$ that $P$ cancels fails the first condition, so that such a tableau is not called A-stable.

Both are exact for a rational T. For a floating-point T, a coefficient of $Q$ counts as zero by the zero count of stability_at_infinity, and a coefficient of $E$ when its magnitude is at most a first-order bound on its round-off: each coefficient of $P$ and $Q$ that is not zero after the count, other than the constant, carries $\mathrm{rtol} \cdot m$, and the products and sums that make $E$ carry these bounds on. The decision is then exact for the remaining coefficients. The default rtol is 16 * eps(T) for a floating-point T and 0 for a rational one. Returns a Bool.

RungeKutta.linear_order — Method
linear_order(tab::Tableau{T}; rtol)

Return the linear order of tab, as an Int: the largest $q$ with $b^T A^{k-1} 𝟙 = 1/k!$ for $k = 1, …, q$. It is the order of the stability function $R(z)$ as an approximation of $\exp(z)$, and so the order of convergence on a linear problem with constant coefficients; the classical order, from the rooted trees, can be lower. It is at most $\deg P + \deg Q ≤ 2s$, because a rational $R$ is not $\exp$, so $k$ runs to $2s$.

A residual counts as zero when $|b^T A^{k-1} 𝟙 - 1/k!| ≤ \mathrm{rtol} \cdot k \cdot |b|^T |A|^{k-1} 𝟙$, with $|\cdot|$ taken entrywise: the first-order bound on the change of $b^T A^{k-1} 𝟙$ when every entry of $A$ and $b$ changes by a relative rtol, which covers the rounding of the stored tableau and the round-off of the products. The default rtol is 16 * eps(T) for a floating-point T and 0 for a rational one, where the test is exact.

RungeKutta.pade_type — Method
pade_type(tab::Tableau{T}; rtol)

Return (deg P, deg Q) where the stability function $R = P/Q$ of tab is the Padé approximation of $\exp(z)$ of that type, that is where the linear_order of tab equals $\deg P + \deg Q$, and nothing otherwise. The degrees are Ints.

The degrees follow from the zero count of stability_at_infinity, and the linear order from its own residual test, both with the same rtol. The default rtol is 16 * eps(T) for a floating-point T and 0 for a rational one.

RungeKutta.real_stability_interval — Method
real_stability_interval(tab::Tableau{T}; rtol) where {T <: Real}

Return the supremum of the $r ≥ 0$ with $|R(x)| ≤ 1$ for every $x ∈ [-r, 0]$, of type float(T), and Inf where no bound exists. A point where $|R| = 1$ and $|R| ≤ 1$ on both sides does not end the interval.

It is decided from the coefficients of $R = P/Q$: the interval ends at the first $u > 0$ where $Q(-u)^2 - P(-u)^2$ changes its sign to negative, found by a square-free factorisation and a Sturm sequence in exact rational arithmetic, and each endpoint is located by bisection to the precision of float(T). For a floating-point T, the coefficients of $P$ and $Q$ are counted as zero as in stability_at_infinity, and a coefficient of $Q(-u)^2 - P(-u)^2$ when its magnitude is at most its first-order bound, as in isastable. For a nonzero rtol, a root where the polynomial changes its sign then ends the interval only where the polynomial, with the factor $u^j$ of its lowest nonzero coefficient divided out, falls below the bound $-\sum_k e_k u^k$ of its round-off at the midpoint between that root and the next, or is negative beyond the last root, so that a touching point that round-off splits into two close roots does not end it. The default rtol is 16 * eps(T) for a floating-point T and 0 for a rational one, where every decision is exact.

Throws an error where the bisection does not locate an endpoint to the precision of float(T) in its step budget, such as the endpoint $2^{-10000}$ of $R(z) = 1 + 2^{10001} z$.

RungeKutta.stability_at_infinity — Method
stability_at_infinity(tab::Tableau{T}; rtol)

Return $R(∞)$, the limit of the stability function $R(z) = P(z)/Q(z)$ of tab as $|z| → ∞$, of type T: the quotient of the leading coefficients of $P$ and $Q$ where their degrees are equal, zero(T) where $\deg P < \deg Q$, and the positive infinity of T where $\deg P > \deg Q$ (1//0 for a rational T). It is computed from the coefficients of stability_function, and does not read the field R∞ of tab.

The degrees follow from a zero count: a coefficient $c$ of $P$ or $Q$ of degree 1 or more counts as zero when $|c| ≤ \mathrm{rtol} \cdot m$, with $m$ the largest magnitude of a coefficient of $P$ and $Q$; the constant coefficients $P(0) = Q(0) = 1$ are exact and never count as zero. The default rtol is 16 * eps(T) for a floating-point T and 0 for a rational one.

RungeKutta.stability_function — Method
stability_function(tab::Tableau{T})

Return the StabilityFunction R of tab: the coefficient vectors R.P and R.Q, of element type T and length $s + 1$, of

\[R(z) = 1 + z b^T (I - z A)^{-1} 𝟙 = \frac{P(z)}{Q(z)} , \qquad P(z) = \det(I - z A + z 𝟙 b^T) , \qquad Q(z) = \det(I - z A) .\]

The coefficients come from the Faddeev–LeVerrier recursion, which uses the ring operations and the division by an integer only. They are exact for a rational element type such as Rational{BigInt} and for a symbolic one, and carry the round-off of that recursion for a floating-point one. No coefficient is trimmed: a coefficient that is zero in exact arithmetic can hold round-off, and the functions that read degrees count it as zero with their keyword rtol.

Throws an ArgumentError when a coefficient of a or b is not finite.

RungeKutta.stability_region — Method
stability_region(tab::Tableau{T}; xlims = (-6.0, 2.0), ylims = (-4.0, 4.0), n = 201)

Return the data of the stability region of tab on a grid of the complex plane, as a NamedTuple (x, y, absR): x and y are the n equally spaced points of the closed intervals xlims and ylims, and absR[i, j] = |R(x[i] + im * y[j])|, with absR[i, j] = Inf wherever $Q(x_i + i y_j) = 0$. All three hold float(T). The stability region is the set where absR ≤ 1.

Throws an ArgumentError when a limit is not finite, when a window is not increasing, or when n < 2; the limits are checked after their conversion to float(T).

RungeKutta.stabilityplot — Function
stabilityplot(tab::Tableau; xlims = (-6.0, 2.0), ylims = (-4.0, 4.0), n = 201)

Plot the stability region of tab in the complex plane: the set where |R(z)| ≤ 1 is filled, its boundary |R(z)| = 1 is drawn as a line, and the real and imaginary axes are drawn as two lines through 0. The data come from stability_region, with the same keywords and defaults, so a plot costs n² evaluations of the stability function. A window or a grid that stability_region refuses raises its ArgumentError.

stabilityplot is a Makie recipe and has a method only when Makie is loaded. A Makie backend, such as CairoMakie or GLMakie, loads Makie and draws the plot. stabilityplot returns a Makie.FigureAxisPlot, and takes the usual figure and axis keywords of Makie:

using CairoMakie
stabilityplot(TableauGauss(2); axis = (; aspect = DataAspect(), xlabel = "Re z", ylabel = "Im z"))

See stabilityplot! to add the plot to an existing axis.

RungeKutta.Tableaus.TableauGauss — Method

Gauss tableau with s stages

TableauGauss(::Type{T}, s)
TableauGauss(s) = TableauGauss(Float64, s)

The constructor takes the number of stages s and optionally the element type T of the tableau.

The nodes and weights are those of the Gauss-Legendre quadrature rule, i.e. the roots of the shifted Legendre polynomial $P_s (2x-1)$ and the corresponding interpolatory weights, taken from QuadratureRules.gauss_legendre_nodes and QuadratureRules.gauss_legendre_weights. The coefficients follow from the simplifying assumption $C(s)$, cf. gauss_coefficients. Prescribing no node leaves all $2s$ parameters free, which is what gives the method its order $2s$.

RungeKutta.Tableaus.TableauIRK3 — Method

Tableau of two-stage, 3rd order fully implicit Runge-Kutta method

TableauIRK3(::Type{T}=Float64) where {T}

The constructor takes one optional argument, that is the element type of the tableau.

RungeKutta.Tableaus.TableauLobattoIIIF — Method

Lobatto IIIF tableau with s stages

TableauLobattoIIIF(::Type{T}, s)
TableauLobattoIIIF(s) = TableauLobattoIIIF(Float64, s)

The constructor takes the number of stages s and optionally the element type T of the tableau.

The stability function of the Lobatto IIIF tableau is the (s, s)-Padé approximation of exp(z), so its linear order, the order of the stability function, is 2s. Its classical order is 2s - 2, because the Lobatto weights satisfy the simplifying assumption B(2s - 2) and not B(2s - 1).

References:

Wang Fangzong and Liao Xiaobing.

RungeKutta.Tableaus.TableauLobattoIIIG — Method

Lobatto IIIG tableau with s stages

TableauLobattoIIIG(::Type{T}, s)
TableauLobattoIIIG(s) = TableauLobattoIIIG(Float64, s)

The constructor takes the number of stages s and optionally the element type T of the tableau.

Symplectizied algorithm for TableauLobattoIIIF

Coefficients are taken as $a^G = \frac{1}{2} ( a^F + \bar{a}^F )$ where the coefficients $\bar{a}^F$ are computed such that the symplecticity conditions $b_{i} \bar{a}_{i,j} + \bar{b}_{j} a_{j,i} = b_{i} \bar{b}_{j}$ and $b_{i} = \bar{b}_i$ hold for all $1 \le i,j \le s$.

The classical order and the linear order of the Lobatto IIIG tableau are both 2s - 2.

RungeKutta.Tableaus.TableauRadauIA — Method

Radau IA tableau with s stages

TableauRadauIA(::Type{T}, s)
TableauRadauIA(s) = TableauRadauIA(Float64, s)

The constructor takes the number of stages s and optionally the element type T of the tableau.

The nodes and weights are those of the left Radau-Legendre quadrature rule, i.e. QuadratureRules.radau_legendre_nodes(T, s, Val(:left)) and the corresponding weights: Radau IA is the family that prescribes the left endpoint, so $c_1 = 0$. Equivalently the nodes are the roots of $\frac{d^{s-1}}{dx^{s-1}} \big( x^s (x-1)^{s-1} \big)$. The coefficients follow from the simplifying assumption $D(s)$, cf. radau_1_coefficients.

Prescribing one endpoint costs one degree of exactness relative to Gauss, giving order $2s-1$. Contrast TableauRadauIIA, which prescribes the right endpoint instead.

References:

Byron Leonard Ehle
On Padé approximations to the exponential function and a-stable methods for the numerical solution of initial value problems.
Research Report CSRR 2010, Dept. AACS, University of Waterloo, 1969.

RungeKutta.Tableaus.TableauRadauIB — Method

Radau IB tableau with s stages

TableauRadauIB(::Type{T}, s)
TableauRadauIB(s) = TableauRadauIB(Float64, s)

The constructor takes the number of stages s and optionally the element type T of the tableau.

Coefficients are taken as $a^B = \frac{1}{2} ( a^A + \bar{a}^A )$ where $a^A$ are the coefficients of the Radau IA method and $\bar{a}^A$ are computed such that the symplecticity conditions $b_{i} \bar{a}_{i,j} + \bar{b}_{j} a_{j,i} = b_{i} \bar{b}_{j}$ and $b_{i} = \bar{b}_i$ hold for all $1 \le i,j \le s$.

Construction of high order symplectic Runge-Kutta methods
Journal of Computational Mathematics, Volume 11, Pages 250-260, 1993.

RungeKutta.Tableaus.TableauRadauIIA — Method

Radau IIA tableau with s stages

TableauRadauIIA(::Type{T}, s)
TableauRadauIIA(s) = TableauRadauIIA(Float64, s)

The constructor takes the number of stages s and optionally the element type T of the tableau.

The nodes and weights are those of the right Radau-Legendre quadrature rule, i.e. QuadratureRules.radau_legendre_nodes(T, s, Val(:right)) and the corresponding weights: Radau IIA is the family that prescribes the right endpoint, so $c_s = 1$. Equivalently the nodes are the roots of $\frac{d^{s-1}}{dx^{s-1}} \big( x^{s-1} (x-1)^s \big)$. The coefficients follow from the simplifying assumption $C(s)$, cf. radau_2_coefficients.

Having the right endpoint among the nodes is what makes the method stiffly accurate, which is why Radau IIA rather than TableauRadauIA is the workhorse for stiff and differential-algebraic problems. Both have order $2s-1$.

\[\sum \limits_{j=1}^{s} a_{ij} c_{j}^{k-1} = \frac{c_i^k}{k} \qquad i = 1 , \, ... , \, s , \; k = 1 , \, ... , \, s .\]

RungeKutta.Tableaus.lobatto_b_coefficients — Method

The Lobatto IIIB coefficients are implicitly given by the so-called simplifying assumption $D(s)$:

\[\sum \limits_{i=1}^{s} b_i c_{i}^{k-1} a_{ij} = \frac{b_j}{k} ( 1 - c_j^k) \qquad j = 1 , \, ... , \, s , \; k = 1 , \, ... , \, s .\]

RungeKutta.Tableaus.lobatto_c_coefficients — Method

The Lobatto IIIC coefficients are determined by setting $a_{i,1} = b_1$ and solving the so-called simplifying assumption $C(s-1)$, given by

\[\sum \limits_{j=1}^{s} a_{ij} c_{j}^{k-1} = \frac{c_i^k}{k} \qquad i = 1 , \, ... , \, s , \; k = 1 , \, ... , \, s-1 ,\]

for $a_{i,j}$ with $i = 1, ..., s$ and $j = 2, ..., s$.

source

for $a_{i,j}$ with $i = 1, ..., s$ and $j = 1, ..., s-1$.

RungeKutta.Tableaus.lobatto_nullvector — Method
lobatto_nullvector(::Type, s)
lobatto_nullvector(s)

Computes the nullvector of the matrix containing the derivatives of the Lagrange basis on the s Lobatto nodes evaluated on these nodes.

The result is of unit length with a positive first entry, cf. _nullvector, so it is determined by the Lobatto nodes alone and not by the factorisation used to obtain it.
